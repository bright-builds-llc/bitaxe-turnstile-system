Privacy

This page describes what this Gate deployment keeps when you use your Bitaxe to earn a Gate Pass. The deployment is three services run by Bright Builds LLC: the Gate Authority, which issues challenges and passes; the Pool Adapter, which connects your miner to the pool; and the Reference Service, the account-creation site you came from. You need no account to use the Gate.

What is kept, and where

  • A key made for this challenge. Your browser makes a new key pair for each challenge and keeps the private half in its own storage, where scripts can't read it. The Gate Authority keeps the public half with the challenge. The Reference Service keeps only a fingerprint of it.
  • A random reference for your action. The Reference Service records the account it created against a random reference, not against your name, email or device.
  • Your work. For each share your miner submits, the Gate Authority and the Pool Adapter keep its difficulty, a fingerprint of it and the time it arrived.
  • Your payout address. The Gate Authority keeps only a one-way commitment to it. The Pool Adapter keeps the address itself while the challenge runs, and it is sent to the pool as the miner's Stratum username, so the pool sees it. The Pool Adapter deletes its copy when the challenge ends, whether it succeeded, was cancelled or expired.
  • Your miner's login. The Pool Adapter gives your miner a generated username and secret for the challenge, and keeps only a keyed check of the secret.
  • Your device. The Gate links leases to a digest of the control session with your Bitaxe. It doesn't record your Bitaxe's network address or hardware address.

The services keep no IP address in their records, and their logs never hold keys, payout data or device identity. [Owner: confirm what your hosting, network and proxy logs record.]

How long it is kept

  • Single-use proofs, Gate Pass bytes and consent receipts become eligible for deletion once they expire.
  • The Gate Authority's challenge, share, payout commitment and issuance records, and the Reference Service's redemption and outcome records, can be reduced at day 30 to a keyed record with no public reference to you, and that record can be deleted at day 90.
  • The Pool Adapter's miner logins and connection records become eligible for deletion 30 days after their session ends. A connection record that never joined a session is deleted when the connection closes or, if the Pool Adapter stopped first, becomes eligible 30 days after the connection opened. Its settled share records become eligible 30 days after the Gate Authority has taken or refused the share; a share record still on its way to the Gate Authority is kept until it arrives.
  • The Reference Service answers a lookup of your action's outcome for 24 hours by default.
  • An account created through the Reference Service is kept until you ask the operator to remove it. The software has no step that removes accounts, so the operator removes it by hand.

These periods are the earliest the software allows. The operator runs the Gate Authority's and the Reference Service's retention steps about once a month, so their eligible records are reduced or deleted at the next monthly run. The Pool Adapter's automatic deletion is on and runs every 24 hours, so its eligible records are deleted within about a day. Apart from that and connection records deleted as their connection closes, the software deletes none of these records by itself.

Who else sees what

The mining pool receives your miner's shares and your payout address, under its own terms. The site you came from receives your Gate Pass, which says your work was done but carries no payout address or share. No other party receives your payout address or shares from the Gate.
